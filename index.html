01 — The boat

A small ship that happens to be a yacht.

This is a full-displacement trawler yacht. She travels at hull speed rather than climbing onto a plane, which is why she can carry a modest 250 hp, burn little at eight knots, and keep her motion predictable when the weather turns.

The hull is welded steel, fabricated by a certified and experienced marine welder — a material chosen for strength, repairability and a working life measured in decades rather than seasons. The design target is ISO/CE Category A, the ocean category: a boat conceived for extended voyages and self-sufficiency, not fair-weather afternoons.

At 44 feet length over all she is comfortable in smaller marinas, and with a low-profile flybridge and a folding radar arch she is designed to be Great Loop compatible. Range approaches 1,000 nautical miles at cruise, so the passage plan can be about where you want to go rather than where the next fuel dock is.

04 — Construction

Steel, prepared properly.

A steel boat lives or dies on two things: the quality of the welding, and what happens to the surface before the first coat goes on. Both are treated as the point of the exercise.

  1. Step 01

    Fabrication

    All steel fabrication is carried out by a certified, experienced marine welder — hull, decks and structure built as a steel vessel, not a moulded one.

  2. Step 02

    White-metal blast

    Abrasive blasting to white metal before coating. That is the surface preparation a rust-inhibiting epoxy system is designed around (SSPC-SP 5 / Sa 3 equivalent).

  3. Step 03

    Epoxy

    AwlGrip rust-inhibiting epoxy coatings build the barrier that keeps oxygen and salt away from the steel.

  4. Step 04

    Urethane topcoat

    An AwlCraft urethane finish for colour, gloss and UV resistance — the part you see, sitting on the part that does the work.

A coating is only as good as the steel beneath it and the preparation in between. Blast, prime and finish are treated as one system, in that order, with nothing skipped.

05 — Cruising ground

Loop-ready by design.

The Great Loop — the inland circuit through the Intracoastal Waterway, the Great Lakes, the rivers and Canada — rewards boats with modest air draft, modest size and long legs between fuel stops. Four decisions on this boat point straight at that trip.

Air draft

Low-profile flybridge

A flybridge adds a fine place to steer from — and a tall thing to hit bridges with. This one is kept deliberately low so the profile stays within inland clearance limits.

Clearance

Folding radar arch

The arch folds to drop the highest point of the boat when a bridge or a lock demands it — and to make the boat easier to move by road or ship.

Size

44 ft length over all

Big enough to live and cruise on for months; small enough for smaller marinas, town docks and locks along the way.

Range

≈1,000 nm legs

Long range at displacement speed means fuel stops are a convenience, not the axis of the itinerary.

A note on “compatible”. Bridge clearances depend on load, ballast, fuel and the final outfit, and they change with the water level. Treat Great Loop compatibility as a design intent: ask for the current air draft, draft and clearance figures before you plan a route.

06 — Questions

The questions buyers actually ask.

What kind of boat is this?

A 44-foot, full-displacement trawler yacht with a welded steel hull, built for long-range coastal and inland cruising rather than planing performance. She is a motor yacht in the small-ship tradition: heavy, simple, and designed to be lived aboard for long stretches.

How fast does it cruise, and how far can it go?

Cruising speed is 8 knots at a quiet 1,800 rpm, with a range approaching 1,000 nautical miles — roughly 125 running hours at cruise speed, or about 1,850 kilometres. That is a passage-planning range, not a sprint figure.

What engine is fitted?

A 250 hp Cummins 6.7-litre marine diesel: a heavy-duty engine warranted for continuous duty and fully certified to US EPA Tier 3 emissions standards. Cummins marine engines are among the most trusted in North America, and service is widely available — a real consideration on a long cruise.

Can it do the Great Loop?

She is designed to be Great Loop compatible: a low-profile flybridge and folding radar arch keep air draft in check, and 44 feet length over all suits smaller marinas and locks. Exact air draft and bridge clearance figures depend on load and outfit — confirm them against the current specification before planning a route.

Is it an ocean-capable boat?

The design target is ISO/CE Category A, the ocean category: extended voyages, significant wave heights, and self-sufficiency in serious conditions. Category A is a design and certification standard rather than a promise about weather — ask for the current compliance status of the boat in build.

How is the steel protected against rust?

All steel fabrication is by a certified, experienced marine welder. After abrasive blasting to white metal, the hull receives a rust-inhibiting AwlGrip epoxy coating system, finished with an AwlCraft urethane topcoat. Preparation, primer and finish are treated as one system.

Can I see the drawings, and what does it cost?

Yes — the current specification, general arrangement drawings and coating schedule are available to prospective owners. Pricing, delivery and specification options are discussed directly, because they depend on the outfit and finish you want. Start with an enquiry.
