# Glossary

> Plain-language definitions for every term used on the page, so an agent or a
> first-time buyer can read the specification without a boating background.

**Last updated:** 2026-10-27

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Air draft** | The height from the waterline to the highest point of the boat. It decides which bridges and overhead obstructions the boat can pass under. Usually quoted with the radar arch up and folded. |
| **AwlCraft** | Awlcraft is Awlgrip's polyurethane topcoat range: the glossy, UV-resistant finish coat. On this boat it is the visible finish over the epoxy system. |
| **AwlGrip (Awlgrip)** | A well-known marine coating brand. On this boat it supplies the rust-inhibiting epoxy coating system applied over the blasted steel. |
| **Beam** | The widest width of the hull, usually quoted in feet or metres. Not yet published for this boat. |
| **Category A (ISO/CE)** | The highest of the four CE recreational craft design categories: “ocean”, for extended voyages with winds above force 8 and significant wave heights above 4 m, where the boat must be largely self-sufficient. It is a design and certification standard; this boat's design target is Category A. |
| **CE marking** | The European conformity marking for recreational craft, applied under the Recreational Craft Directive/Regulation. Category A/B/C/D describes the sea conditions a design is intended for. |
| **Continuous duty** | An engine rating intended for running at load for unlimited hours, as opposed to an intermittent or light-duty rating. The Cummins engine here is warranted for continuous duty. |
| **Cummins 6.7 L** | The 6.7-litre inline-six marine diesel used here, rated at 250 hp. A common, widely serviced engine family. |
| **Displacement hull** | A hull that floats by displacing water and travels at “hull speed”, pushing water aside rather than climbing onto a plane. Efficient and seakindly at low speed; it does not go fast. |
| **Draft (draught)** | The depth of water needed to float the boat, measured from the waterline to the lowest point of the hull or running gear. Not yet published for this boat. |
| **EPA Tier 3** | US Environmental Protection Agency emission standards for marine diesel engines. “Fully certified to US EPA Tier 3” means the engine meets that standard. |
| **Flybridge** | An upper steering station on the roof of the deckhouse, usually with duplicate controls. A “low-profile” flybridge keeps air draft down. |
| **Full displacement** | A hull designed to operate only in displacement mode — no planing speed, no compromise for it. The type is chosen for range and seaworthiness. |
| **Great Loop** | The ~6,000-mile inland circuit of eastern North America: the Intracoastal Waterway, the Great Lakes, the Mississippi and other rivers, and Canada. Key constraints are bridge air draft, draft, and boat length for locks and small marinas. |
| **Hull speed** | The practical maximum speed of a displacement hull, roughly 1.34 × the square root of the waterline length in feet. Pushing beyond it consumes power for little gain. |
| **ISO/CE** | The International Organization for Standardization and the European conformity framework; “ISO/CE Category A” is shorthand for the Category A design standard under the recreational craft rules. |
| **Kn (knot)** | One nautical mile per hour. 1 kn ≈ 1.852 km/h ≈ 1.151 mph. |
| **LOA (length over all)** | The extreme length of the boat, including bowsprits, pulpits and swim platforms. Published here as 44 ft (13.4 m). |
| **Nm (nautical mile)** | 1,852 metres, or about 1.151 statute miles. Used for marine distances. |
| **Planing hull** | A hull that rises onto the surface of the water at speed, trading efficiency and seakeeping for speed. The opposite of this boat's approach. |
| **Radar arch** | The arch over the flybridge or cabin that carries radar, antennas and lights. A folding arch lowers those fittings to cut air draft. |
| **RPM** | Revolutions per minute of the engine. This boat cruises at 1,800 rpm. |
| **SSPC-SP 5 / Sa 3** | Abrasive blast cleaning standards for “white metal” — the highest surface cleanliness grade, with all rust, mill scale and coating removed. Referenced here as the intent behind “sandblasted to white metal”. |
| **Trawler yacht** | A motor yacht styled after a fishing trawler: displacement hull, modest power, long range, generous accommodation, often with a raised pilothouse or flybridge. Built for cruising, not speed. |
| **White metal** | The bare, uniformly grey steel surface left after a full abrasive blast, before any coating. |
