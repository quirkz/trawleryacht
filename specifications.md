## Propulsion

A **250 hp Cummins 6.7 L marine diesel** is fitted.

- **Duty rating:** heavy duty, **warranted for continuous duty** — an engine rating
  intended for long hours at load, not a light-duty sprint rating.
- **Emissions:** fully certified to **US EPA Tier 3** standards.
- **Support:** Cummins marine engines are among the most trusted in North America,
  with service widely available — a practical consideration on a long cruise.
- **Cruise:** **8 knots at 1,800 rpm**, described as quiet.

## Construction and finish

1. **Fabrication.** All steel fabrication is by a certified, experienced marine welder.
2. **Surface preparation.** Abrasive blasting to **white metal** before coating —
   the preparation a rust-inhibiting epoxy system is designed around. White metal is
   equivalent in intent to SSPC-SP 5 / NACE 1 / Sa 3.
3. **Epoxy.** **AwlGrip** rust-inhibiting epoxy coatings build the barrier against
   oxygen and salt.
4. **Topcoat.** **AwlCraft** urethane finish for colour, gloss and UV resistance.

A steel vessel's coating life is governed by preparation as much as by product. The
builder treats blast, prime and finish as one system, in that order.

## Design intent: Great Loop

The Great Loop is the inland circuit through the Intracoastal Waterway, the Great
Lakes, the rivers and Canada. It rewards modest air draft, modest size and long legs
between fuel stops.

| Design decision | Effect on the Loop |
| --- | --- |
| Low-profile flybridge | Keeps the highest point of the boat within inland bridge clearances |
| Folding radar arch | Drops air draft further when a bridge, lock or road move demands it |
| 44 ft LOA | Fits smaller marinas, town docks and locks |
| Range approaching 1,000 nm | Fuel stops become a convenience rather than the itinerary |

Great Loop compatibility is a **design intent**, not a surveyed guarantee: clearances
depend on load, ballast, fuel and final outfit. Confirm air draft, draft and bridge
clearance against the current specification before planning a route.
